Abhilash KurapatiTechnical interview prep, 30 min with the hiring manager # Every question your resume invites, answered. Start with the questions marked likely. Read the answer, say it out loud in your own words, then mark it confident or review again. **0**questions **0**confident **0**to review **Adapt before you speak.** Answers are built from your resume. Where they suggest specifics you didn't do (a tool, a metric, a step), replace them with what really happened. A manager will follow up on anything you say. ## Topics Topic From 0:00 Answer out loud before revealing. Aim for 60 to 90 seconds. Keys: Space show answer, G got it, R review again, → skip. ## Numbers on your resume Every number invites "how did you measure that?" Know what each one means and the one-line explanation behind it. ## How the 30 minutes will probably go Manager interviews usually mix your story, one or two project deep dives, a design discussion, and time for your questions. Use this to pace yourself. 1. 0–3 min ### Introductions and your background Deliver the 90-second story. End by pointing toward the J&J project so they ask about it. 2. 3–13 min ### Deep dive on your current project Architecture, hallucination controls, HITL, the turnaround metric. Draw the system if there's a whiteboard or shared screen. 3. 13–20 min ### Technical breadth RAG, agents versus workflows, MCP, GCP choices, testing and CI/CD. Short answers; let them pick what to dig into. 4. 20–26 min ### Design or scenario question Clarify requirements, sketch components, name trade-offs, mention evaluation and cost. 5. 26–30 min ### Your questions Ask two thoughtful questions. Thank them and restate your interest in one sentence. ### Do - Lead with the answer, then give detail. - Say "I" for your work and "we" for the team's, and be clear which is which. - Name trade-offs: what you chose, what you gave up. - Pause and ask "want me to go deeper on any part?" - Say "I haven't done that, but here's how I'd approach it" when true. ### Avoid - Answers over two minutes without checking in. - Claiming you ran RLHF training when it was a feedback loop. - Sharing confidential J&J details or client names beyond your resume. - Framework lists without the reason you chose each. - Speaking negatively about a past employer. ### Tonight - Go through every "Likely" question once. - Write your real 5+ workflow steps and your real 4 ranker signals. - Draw the J&J architecture from memory in two minutes. ### Interview day - Drill "Needs review" for 20 minutes. - Reread the job description and pick two questions for the manager. - Test your camera, mic and a whiteboard tool.
